\section{Measured yeast ribosome-density endpoint}

To move beyond an optimizer scoring its own objective, native yeast CDS sequences were joined to public GSE75897 RPF and RiboZero RPKM measurements. The endpoint is log2(RPF/RNA), a ribosome-density ratio, not protein yield or initiation efficiency. Of the reference CDS records, 5,068 unique locus tags had positive measurements and valid coding sequence. Five shuffled folds estimate prediction on withheld genes. Standardization and RidgeCV regularization selection are fitted within each training fold.

\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{lrr}\hline Feature view & OOF Pearson $r$ & OOF MSE\\\hline

length\_gc & 0.420 & 1.005 \\

codon\_freq & 0.536 & 0.870 \\

codon\_length\_gc & 0.572 & 0.821 \\

\hline\end{tabular}\end{center}

Codon composition adds predictive information beyond length and GC in this measured native-sequence endpoint. The combined model improves OOF Pearson correlation from 0.420 to 0.572. This is an association, not evidence that changing codons causes the predicted change. Homologous genes may cross folds, only one measurement pair is used, and the density ratio does not correct differential elongation or common measurement noise. No E. coli optimizer transfer, yeast redesign benefit, or new translation mechanism is established.

This supplies a second-organism measured-endpoint audit, while direct expression validation of optimized designs remains open. Source: \url{https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE75897}. The archived files, CDS provenance, included locus tags and hashes make the join reproducible.

\subsection{Separating synonymous association from amino-acid composition}

Raw codon frequencies mix amino-acid composition with synonymous choice. A more specific audit decomposes each sequence into 20 amino-acid fractions and 61 within-amino-acid codon fractions, excluding termination codons. The comparison holds amino-acid composition, length and GC available in both arms. Five chromosome-disjoint folds prevent the same chromosome from entering both training and evaluation; standardization and regularization selection use training data only. The 5,068 matched genes span 16 reference chromosomes.

\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{lrr}\hline Feature view & OOF Pearson $r$ & OOF MSE\\\hline

length\_gc & 0.420 & 1.005 \\

aa\_length\_gc & 0.526 & 0.883 \\

aa\_syn\_length\_gc & 0.569 & 0.826 \\

\hline\end{tabular}\end{center}

Adding synonymous fractions reduces paired out-of-fold mean squared error by 0.0569. A conditional chromosome-cluster bootstrap gives a 95\% interval of 0.0457 to 0.0733.

This positive result is narrower than an expression-improvement claim: synonymous composition adds predictive information for a measured ribosome-density ratio beyond the amino-acid/length/GC baseline in this dataset. It does not prove that changing synonymous codons causes the observed difference. Unmeasured expression regulation, gene-family dependence, ratio noise and experimental-strain/reference mismatch can still confound interpretation. Chromosome holdouts are not homolog-family holdouts. The bootstrap conditions on fitted out-of-fold predictions rather than repeating model fitting.

A falsifiable next step is family-disjoint validation followed by experimentally measured synonymous redesigns. Failure at either step would withdraw the intervention claim, not erase the present association. Full fold chromosome assignments, metrics and scope are recorded in results/yeast\_synonymous\_specificity.json.
\subsection{Protein-similarity group separation and nested tuning}

A follow-up tests whether the synonymous association survives separation of close protein-similarity groups. The same 5,068 native CDS with measured positive RPF and RNA abundance are translated using the standard genetic code. CD-HIT version 4.8.1 is compiled from unmodified source commit \texttt{4f6720f573d3d9d4}\allowbreak\texttt{c835793a05f09e098003bfe9}. The clustering threshold is 50\% global identity, defined by the tool as identical aligned residues divided by the full shorter-sequence length. Alignment must cover at least 80\% of both sequences. Accurate representative assignment is enabled. This produces 4,834 clusters, including 213 non-singleton clusters, with a largest cluster of five genes. These are representative-based similarity groups, not an exhaustive assignment of homolog families.

Five outer group-disjoint folds keep every member of a protein cluster in the same test fold. Four inner group-disjoint folds select ridge regularization from 0.1, 1, 10, 100 and 1,000 by mean squared error. Standardization is fitted separately inside each inner training split, then the selected pipeline is refitted to the outer training set. Neither the test outcomes nor test membership labels enter parameter fitting. Clustering uses sequences, not the abundance target. This is a stricter tuning design than the earlier RidgeCV pilot and is retained as a separate audit rather than replacing it.

\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{lrr}\hline
Feature view & OOF Pearson $r$ & OOF MSE\\\hline
Length and GC & 0.420 & 1.0054\\
Amino acids, length and GC & 0.523 & 0.8862\\
Amino acids, synonymous fractions, length and GC & 0.568 & 0.8275\\\hline
\end{tabular}\end{center}

Adding within-amino-acid synonymous fractions reduces paired out-of-fold mean squared error by 0.05870. A protein-cluster bootstrap, conditional on these fitted out-of-fold predictions, gives a 95\% interval of 0.04450 to 0.07281. Resampling takes whole clusters with replacement, preserving members together, and computes the gene-weighted mean loss difference. It does not refit the nested models or estimate biological replicate uncertainty. Fold assignments, selected regularization, locus-to-cluster mapping, target values and all out-of-fold predictions are saved so the result can be independently recomputed.

This supports the narrower statement that synonymous composition adds predictive information for a measured native-sequence ribosome-density endpoint even after this close-protein-similarity separation. It does not establish causal effects of synonymous redesign, protein-yield improvement, optimized-sequence benefit, or transfer to another species. The most common clusters remain singletons, so the holdout changes the handling of 447 genes in non-singleton groups rather than proving all related genes are isolated. Distant homologs, domain-sharing genes and cross-cluster pairs can still cross folds: CD-HIT assigns against representatives and is not an all-pairs connected-component family algorithm. Its identity and coverage cutoffs are methodological choices, not biological family boundaries.

Unmeasured regulation, reference-versus-experimental-strain differences, ratio measurement noise and elongation effects remain. A positive conditional bootstrap interval cannot remove those confounds. The endpoint is one public RPF/RNA measurement pair, not a controlled synonymously redesigned library. The existing chromosome-separated result and this protein-cluster result are complementary leakage controls, not independent biological replications of the same experiment.

Reproduction uses \texttt{scripts/yeast\_protein\_cluster\_specificity.py} with a compiled \texttt{--cdhit} path. Protein FASTA, representative output, complete cluster file and command log are retained in the data and results directories. The original GEO and RefSeq abundance/CDS files are unchanged. Clustering semantics are documented at \url{https://github.com/weizhongli/cdhit/blob/master/doc/cdhit-user-guide.wiki}; the source build is \url{https://github.com/weizhongli/cdhit}. Experimental synonymous redesign remains the next intervention test, with a null result preserved if prediction fails to translate into measured expression.
